\subsection{System and Model}

The experimental setup is based on the Quanser SRV-02 DC servomotor, a laboratory platform designed for motion-control experiments. The system consists of a DC motor, a voltage driver, a gearbox with reduction ratio ($N$), and position sensing devices. The voltage driver supplies the motor armature, while the gearbox increases the output torque and reduces the shaft speed.

The control input of the system is the voltage command (u(t)) generated by the controller. This signal is applied to the voltage driver, which behaves as a first-order low-pass filter. Consequently, the motor current is smoothed and high-frequency components are attenuated before reaching the motor armature.

The plant output is the load angular position ($\vartheta_l(t)$). This quantity is measured by a potentiometer mechanically coupled to the output shaft through an anti-backlash gear mechanism. The potentiometer therefore provides a direct measurement of the load position used for feedback control.

Neglecting the high-frequency dynamics associated with the voltage driver and the electrical circuit, the relationship between the input voltage (u(t)) and the load angular position ($\vartheta_l(t)$) is described by the transfer function
$$
P(s)=\frac{\Theta_l(s)}{U(s)}
=\frac{k_m}{sN\left(T_m s+1\right)},
$$
where (U(s)) and ($\Theta_l(s)$) are the Laplace transforms of the input voltage and load position, respectively.

The parameter ($k_m$) denotes the motor gain, while ($T_m$) is the mechanical time constant:
$$
k_m=\frac{k_{drv}k_t}{R_{eq}B_{eq}+k_tk_e},
\qquad
T_m=\frac{R_{eq}J_{eq}}{R_{eq}B_{eq}+k_tk_e}.
$$
Here, ($k_{drv}$) is the voltage-driver gain, ($k_t$) is the motor torque constant, ($k_e$) is the back-electromotive-force constant, ($J_{eq}$) is the equivalent inertia, ($B_{eq}$) is the equivalent viscous friction coefficient, and ($R_{eq}$) is the equivalent armature resistance.

A state-space representation of the plant is obtained by choosing the state vector

$$
x(t)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\vartheta_l(t)\\
\omega_l(t)
\end{bmatrix},
$$

where ($\vartheta_l(t)$) is the load angular position and ($\omega_l(t)$) is the load angular velocity.

The resulting continuous-time state-space model is

$$
\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t),
\qquad
y(t)=Cx(t)+Du(t),
$$

with

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 1\\
0 & -\dfrac{1}{T_m}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
\dfrac{k_m}{N\,T_m}
\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
C=
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
D=0.
$$
